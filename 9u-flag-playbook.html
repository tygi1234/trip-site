Coach Tyler · 9-Year-Old Flag Football

9U Flag Playbook

Three practices before Game 1 — built for fun first, fundamentals second, plays third. Print any tab or work off your phone at the field.

How this is built

Everything below assumes standard 9U rec-league flag rules. Check your league's actual rule sheet — if anything's different, tell me and I'll rebuild the timing.

Assumptions baked in: 5-on-5 (1 center/snapper, 1 QB, 3 eligible receivers), no blocking or contact, one-hand or flag-belt pull instead of a tackle, a 4–5 second rush count before a rusher can cross the line, all offensive players are eligible to catch a pass, and a 60–70 minute practice window. Swap in your real numbers any time — the drills scale fine to 6–7 players a side.

The season goal

01
Kids have fun and want to come back
02
Everyone can catch, throw, and pull a flag safely
03
Team knows 4–5 plays cold by Game 1
04
Equal reps and playtime for every kid

Coaching reminders

  • Praise effort, not just results. At this age, attention span and confidence matter more than execution.
  • Water every 10–12 minutes, more if it's hot. Don't wait for kids to ask.
  • Keep instructions to one idea at a time. "Catch it with two hands" beats a four-part technique lecture.
  • Every drill should have a line of 3 or fewer. Standing in line is where 9-year-olds lose interest — run stations, not one line for the whole team.
  • End on something fun, win or lose the scrimmage — a relay, a trick play, a team cheer.

Default 65-minute practice structure

Use this shell for every practice after the first one — just swap what's being taught in the "install" and "scrimmage" blocks. Trim proportionally if your field time runs short.

0:00

Arrival warm-up game

8 min

A tag-based game that sneaks in conditioning — see "Flag Tag" in Practice 1. Start every practice this way; it's the hook that gets kids running before they realize it's a warm-up.

0:08

Dynamic stretch & agility

7 min

High knees, butt kicks, lateral shuffles, backpedal, two lines through an agility ladder or cone gate if you have one. Keep it moving — no static stretching at this age.

0:15

Skill stations (rotate in 3 small groups)

18 min

Split the team into 3 groups of 2–3 kids, 6 minutes per station: (A) throwing & catching, (B) flag pulling & footwork, (C) ball security / handoffs. Small groups = more touches per kid.

0:33

Team install — offense & defense

15 min

Walk through that week's play(s) at half speed with no defense, then add light "dummy" defense once they've got it. This is where the Playbook tab comes in.

0:48

Scrimmage / situational reps

14 min

Split into two mini-teams and play live. Stop and reset if something needs fixing — don't let bad habits run for a full possession.

1:02

Water, cheer, recap

3 min

One thing they did well as a team, one thing to work on, team cheer, dismiss.

Total≈ 65 minutes
Special — First Practice

Practice 1: Meet, Move, and Measure

Goals: learn names, make it fun immediately, teach the two rules that matter most (how the flag pull works, one-hand touch), and quietly assess where each kid is skill-wise. No real plays yet — just enough structure to end on a confidence win.

0:00

Welcome circle

5 min

Names, "what position do you want to try," and the two rules: pull the flag / one-hand touch instead of tackling, and no blocking or grabbing jerseys. Keep it upbeat — this is a sales pitch for why flag football is awesome.

0:05

Flag Tag (warm-up game)

8 min

Everyone wears a flag belt inside a marked square. On "go," everyone tries to pull others' flags while keeping their own. Last flag standing wins a round. Teaches flag-pulling instinct and open-field movement without ever calling it a drill.

0:13

Dynamic stretch & agility

5 min

High knees, butt kicks, side shuffle, two short sprints.

0:18

Skill assessment stations

25 min

3 groups, ~8 min each, rotate: (A) Throw & Catch — soft toss at 5–8 yards, watch grip and hands; (B) Sprint & Flag Pull — 15-yard chase drill, watch angle and closing speed; (C) Handoff & Ball Security — walk-through handoffs, watch if they carry with two hands. Jot quick notes on the checklist below as you go — you don't need to say anything to the kids about it.

0:43

Learn to line up + first play

10 min

Teach the base formation (see Playbook tab) and walk through Slant Right five or six times, rotating who plays QB. Goal is just "everyone knows where to stand," not execution.

0:53

Flag-pull relay (fun finisher)

10 min

Two lines race one at a time to pull a flag off a coach or cone-dummy and sprint back. Loud, silly, competitive — send them home excited.

1:03

Team cheer & wrap-up

2 min

One shared cheer, remind them about next practice, high-fives on the way out.

Total≈ 65 minutes

Practice 2

Practice 2: Build the Playbook

Goals: sharpen the fundamentals from Practice 1, install two more plays, and introduce the idea of defense for the first time.

0:00

Flag Tag warm-up

8 min

Same game as Practice 1 — repetition builds the habit. Add a rule: no running outside the square, to sharpen change-of-direction.

0:08

Dynamic stretch & agility

7 min

Add a simple cone weave to last week's warm-up.

0:15

Skill stations

15 min

3 groups: (A) Catching in stride — thrown slightly ahead so they run through it; (B) Flag-pull angles — approaching from the side, not head-on; (C) Snap-and-go — center-to-QB exchange.

0:30

Install: Sweep Right & Middle Screen

15 min

Walk through both new plays at half speed (see Playbook tab), then review Slant Right from last week so all three are fresh.

0:45

Intro to defense

10 min

Teach Man Coverage: each defender lines up across from one eligible receiver. One player rushes the QB after counting to 5 out loud ("Mississippi" counts work great and self-police the rush count).

0:55

Live scrimmage

15 min

Split into two mini-squads, offense vs. defense, rotate who's on offense every 3–4 plays so everyone gets both sides.

1:10

Water, cheer, recap

3 min

Highlight one good rep from the scrimmage by name.

Total≈ 73 minutes
Practice 3 — Pre-Game

Practice 3: Game Week

Goals: lock in the full playbook, rehearse real game situations, assign starting jobs, and send them into Game 1 confident instead of overloaded with new information.

0:00

Quick warm-up game

5 min

Shortened Flag Tag — save time for game-speed work.

0:05

Dynamic stretch

5 min

Keep it brief and familiar; nothing new this practice.

0:10

Full playbook walkthrough

15 min

Run all four plays — Slant Right, Sweep Right, Middle Screen, Post Pass — at half speed, no defense. Everyone should be able to say their own job out loud before you speed it up.

0:25

Situational drills

15 min

Set up specific game moments: "last play before halftime," "need a big gain," "protect a short lead." Teaches decision-making, not just play-running.

0:40

Live scrimmage — real game rules

20 min

Run it exactly like Game 1 will go: coin toss, series, both Man Coverage defense and the offensive plays. This is the dress rehearsal.

1:00

Team meeting

10 min

Confirm who's starting where, review sideline behavior and the flag-belt check, answer questions, and end with the team cheer. Keep the tone: "we're ready and this is going to be fun."

Total≈ 70 minutes

Base formation

Same starting alignment for every play below, so the only thing that changes is what happens after the snap: Center (C) snaps to the Quarterback (QB); X and Z line up wide on either side; Y lines up in the slot. No blocking is allowed — receivers create space by running away from defenders, not by engaging them.

Offense — 4 plays for Game 1

Play 1 · Quick Pass

Slant Right

Z cuts sharply toward the middle 5 yards past the line; X runs the same slant on the opposite side as a decoy. QB throws quick to whichever slant comes open.

Coach's point: This is the play they learn first — quick release, easy read, fast confidence win.

Play 2 · Pitch & Run

Sweep Right

QB pitches back to Y, who's already moving right at the snap. X and Z both sprint straight downfield to pull their defenders away and clear the sideline for Y.

Coach's point: No blocking, so the "block" is really just X and Z running their defenders far away from the play.

Play 3 · Safety Valve

Middle Screen

Y sneaks a few yards past the line and turns to face the QB for a short catch, while X and Z run straight downfield to clear defenders out of the middle.

Coach's point: The "easy button" play — use it when a kid needs a confidence-building catch.

Play 4 · Deep Shot

Post Pass

Z sprints deep and angles toward the middle; X runs a short out route as a check-down. Save this one for when a defender is playing close.

Coach's point: Only call this once the team is comfortable with the other three — it needs the most timing.

Defense — 2 concepts

Defense A

Man Coverage

Each defender lines up across from one eligible receiver and mirrors them. One defender rushes the QB, counting "one-Mississippi... five-Mississippi" out loud before crossing the line.

Coach's point: Simplest defense to teach — "stay with your guy" is one instruction every kid can follow.

Defense B

Soft Zone

Three defenders drop into short-left, short-right, and deep-middle areas instead of guarding one kid. One rushes on the count, one "spies" the QB in case they run.

Coach's point: Introduce this only after Man Coverage feels comfortable — it needs more field awareness.
