9U Flag Playbook

Three practices before Game 1 — built for fun first, fundamentals second, plays third. Print any tab or work off your phone at the field.

How this is built

Two coaches on the field, 9–10 year olds, playing under Neighborhood Sports NFL Flag rules. Everything below — station counts, drills, plays — is built around exactly two adults running practice.

Confirmed from the Neighborhood Sports 9–10 division rulebook: 5-on-5, every possession starts at your own 5-yard line (no live kickoff to cover), 3 downs to reach midfield for a first down and 3 more to score, no running inside the 5-yard "No Rush Zones" before midfield or the end zone (pass only in there), rushers must line up 7 yards off the ball with a max of 2 blitzers who raise a hand pre-snap, zero contact allowed with the QB, and no fumbles — the ball is simply down where it hits the ground. Full breakdown below.

League rules cheat sheet

Pulled straight from the Neighborhood Sports Code of Conduct & Rule Book. Double check with your specific field director in case your division runs a variant.

Player rules

  • First down: 3 downs to cross midfield, then 3 more downs to score. No punting once you're past midfield — if you don't score in 3 downs, the other team takes over at their own 5.
  • No Rush Zones: the 5 yards before midfield and the 5 yards before the end zone are pass-only. No running plays inside them — 9–10 division can run anywhere else on the field.
  • Rushing the QB: up to 2 defenders can blitz, but they must line up 7 yards off the ball and raise a hand before the snap to identify themselves. No delayed blitzes, and no contact with the QB — it's a flag pull or a batted ball only.
  • QB clock: 7 seconds to hand off, pitch, or throw beyond the line of scrimmage. The QB can never run it himself.
  • No fumbles: if the ball hits the ground, it's simply down at that spot — no scramble for it.
  • Scoring: touchdown = 6, then choose a 1-point conversion from the 5-yard line or a 2-point conversion from the 10. A defensive interception returned for a score is automatically 2 points.
  • No live kickoff: every new possession — start of a half, after a score, after a turnover past midfield — begins at your own 5-yard line.

Coach rules

  • One coach on the field during a play, staying at least 5 yards behind the deepest player — no running alongside the play.
  • Only the head coach may speak to the referee, and only during a timeout, on the sideline.
  • Play every kid at least 50% of the game — referees are watching for this, no exceptions.
  • Handshake line after every game, win or lose. Zero tolerance for taunting or profanity from anyone on the sideline.
  • 3 timeouts per game — the only way to stop the clock.

Source: Neighborhood Sports Code of Conduct + Rule Book.

Coaching reminders

  • Praise effort, not just results. At this age, attention span and confidence matter more than execution.
  • Water every 10–12 minutes, more if it's hot. Don't wait for kids to ask.
  • Keep instructions to one idea at a time. "Catch it with two hands" beats a four-part technique lecture.
  • Two coaches, two stations, max. Every drill below is built for exactly two groups running at once — no third station to spread yourselves across.
  • End on something fun, win or lose the scrimmage — a relay, a trick play, a team cheer.

Use this shell for every practice after the first one — just swap what's being taught in the "install" and "scrimmage" blocks. Trim proportionally if your field time runs short. Built for 2 coaches running 2 stations.

High knees, butt kicks, lateral shuffles, backpedal, two lines through an agility ladder or cone gate if you have one. Keep it moving — no static stretching at this age. See the Skills tab for the "Dynamic Warm-Up Lane" visual.

5 min

Names, "what position do you want to try," and the two rules for today: two-hand touch instead of tackling (flags come later once they're issued), and no blocking or grabbing jerseys. Keep it upbeat — this is a sales pitch for why flag football is awesome.

25 min

Skills & drills

Every station referenced in the practice plans, with a diagram and a one-line coaching cue. Built as 2 stand-alone drills per skill area so two coaches can each own one at a time.

Skill · Warm-Up

Dynamic Warm-Up Lane

One 15–20 yard lane, four movements back to back: high knees, butt kicks, a lateral shuffle (face the same way the whole time), then a backpedal. Two lines go at once so nobody waits long.

Cue: "Quick feet, chest tall." No static stretching at this age — moving is the warm-up.

Skill · Throw & Catch

5-Yard Pitch & Catch

Partners face each other 5–8 yards apart and throw back and forth — one knee-high, one chest-high, one thrown slightly to a side so they have to move. Rotate partners every few minutes.

Cue: "Point your front shoulder at your target, step and throw." For catching: "hands out like a triangle, look it into your hands."

Skill · Catch on the Move

Gauntlet Catch

One kid jogs a straight lane while two coaches toss soft passes into the lane from either side, one after another, without the runner stopping.

Cue: "Catch it, tuck it, two hands." Teaches catching in stride instead of stopping to catch — what game reps actually look like.

Skill · Flag Pull

Angle Chase

Ball carrier jogs an easy diagonal across a 10-yard lane. The defender starts a step behind and to the side and pursues from the angle — never straight on from behind.

Cue: "Take the angle, not the chase." Approaching from the hip is faster and safer than a head-on, tackle-style approach.

Skill · No Flags Yet

Two-Hand Touch Chase

Same setup as Angle Chase, before flags are handed out: ball carrier jogs an easy diagonal, defender pursues from the angle, and finishes with a two-hand touch on the back or shoulders instead of a pull.

Cue: "Two hands, one touch, done." Use this version for Practice 1 — switch to Angle Chase once flag belts are issued.

Skill · Defensive Flag Pull

Break Down & Pull

Ball carrier jogs a zig-zag path changing direction 2–3 times. The defender closes most of the distance at speed, then breaks down into short, choppy steps under control before reaching for the flag.

Cue: "Speed up, then chop down." Diving or lunging for the flag is what causes whiffs and trips — under control, they'll get there almost every time.

Skill · Defensive Positioning

Mirror & Shadow

Receiver jogs side to side across a 10-yard lane. Defender mirrors every cut a half-step behind and slightly toward the end zone — never lets the receiver get on the goal-line side of them.

Cue: "Keep him between you and the end zone." If he beats you, he beats you toward the sideline, never deep.

Skill · Ball Security

Handoff Gate

QB and running back jog toward each other through two cone "gates" set 5 yards apart, exchanging a clean handoff right at the midpoint.

Cue for the runner: "Two hands until it's tucked, then high and tight." Cue for the QB: "Place it in the pocket, don't throw the handoff."

Skill · Footwork

4-Cone Box Shuffle

Set 4 cones in a 5x5 yard square. Kids shuffle sideways around the whole box without crossing their feet, staying low the entire way around.

Cue: "Stay low, quick feet, chest over knees." Same footwork they'll use to mirror a receiver or change direction chasing a flag.

Fun & games

Skill-building disguised as games. Use these for warm-ups, finishers, or anytime energy is dropping — every one of them is secretly a football drill.

Fun · No-Equipment Warm-Up

Blob Tag

One or two kids start as "it" with no flags needed — just a two-hand touch to tag. Anyone tagged joins hands with the tagger, growing into a "blob" that must stay linked while chasing. Last untagged kid wins.

Why it works: perfect for Practice 1 before flags are issued — zero equipment, and the shrinking open space still teaches evasive running and change-of-direction.

Fun · Warm-Up

Flag Tag

Everyone wears their flag belt inside a marked square. On "go," everyone tries to pull others' flags while protecting their own. Last flag standing wins the round — reset and go again.

Why it works: zero setup beyond the flags they already wear, and it teaches flag-pull instinct and change-of-direction without ever feeling like a drill. Once flags are issued, this replaces Blob Tag as the go-to warm-up.

Fun · Relay

Flag-Pull Relay

Two lines race one at a time to sprint down, pull the flag off a coach or cone-dummy, and sprint back to tag the next kid in line.

Why it works: loud, competitive, and every kid gets a clear individual turn to succeed in front of the team. No flags yet? Swap the pull for a two-hand touch on the shoulder — same relay, same energy.

Fun · Race

Gauntlet Sprint

Same setup as the Gauntlet Catch drill on the Skills tab, but run it as a head-to-head race between two kids in side-by-side lanes — first one through with a clean catch wins.

Why it works: turns a skill rep into a competition kids ask to run again.

Fun · Listening & Agility

Coach Says

Simon Says with football movements: call out "high knees," "backpedal," "freeze," or "flag down" and kids react instantly. Anyone who reacts to the wrong call, or reacts too slow, sits out one round.

Why it works: builds the same quick-reaction listening they'll need for snap counts and coverage calls, disguised as a game.

Fun · Team Spirit

Who Dat Chant

Huddle up before kickoff or after every practice. Coach calls "Who dat?!" and the whole team yells back "Who dat say dey gonna beat dem Saints!" — finish with a team clap-break.

Why it works: takes 15 seconds, gives the team an identity of their own, and it's loud enough to end any practice on a high note.

Base formation

Same starting alignment for every play below, so the only thing that changes is what happens after the snap: Center (C) snaps to the Quarterback (QB); X and Z line up wide on either side; Y lines up in the slot. No blocking is allowed — receivers create space by running away from defenders, not by engaging them.

Offensive passes

The 5-yard No Rush Zones before midfield and before the end zone are pass-only, so most of this offense's yardage comes through the air. These favor short, safety-valve, and comeback-style routes — receivers working back toward the QB catch far more reliably at this age than ones racing away from him.

Play · Quick Pass

Slant Right

Z cuts sharply toward the middle 5 yards past the line; X runs the same slant on the opposite side as a decoy. QB throws quick to whichever slant comes open.

Coach's point: This is the play they learn first — quick release, easy read, fast confidence win.

Play · Safety Valve

Middle Screen

Y sneaks a few yards past the line and turns to face the QB for a short catch, while X and Z run straight downfield to clear defenders out of the middle.

Coach's point: The "easy button" play — use it when a kid needs a confidence-building catch.

Play · Comeback

Curl

Z sprints downfield about 8 yards, plants, and curls back toward the QB. X runs a shorter slant on the other side as a decoy and second option.

Coach's point: Replaces a straight deep post at this age — kids catch a ball coming toward them far more reliably than one they're racing away from.

Play · Crossing Route

Drag Across the Middle

X runs a shallow route straight across the field just past the line, staying low and available the whole way. Z clears out deep to open the space. QB can hit X in stride, or let him settle and turn back for an easy pitch-and-catch.

Coach's point: One of the easiest completions to teach — X is moving toward open grass and can always turn back to the QB, no need for a perfectly timed deep throw.

Offensive runs

Remember it's pass-only inside both No Rush Zones (5 yards before midfield, 5 yards before the end zone) — save this one for open field between them.

Play · Pitch & Run

Sweep Right

QB pitches back to Y, who's already moving right at the snap. X and Z both sprint straight downfield to pull their defenders away and clear the sideline for Y.

Coach's point: No blocking, so the "block" is really just X and Z running their defenders far away from the play.

Defense — 2 concepts

Defense A

Man Coverage

Each defender lines up across from one eligible receiver and mirrors them. Up to 2 defenders can rush from 7 yards off the ball, raising a hand pre-snap to identify themselves — no delayed blitzes, no contact with the QB.

Coach's point: Simplest defense to teach — "stay with your guy" is one instruction every kid can follow.

Defense B

Soft Zone

Three defenders drop into short-left, short-right, and deep-middle areas instead of guarding one kid. One rushes from 7 yards off the ball, one "spies" the QB in case he tries to scramble.

Coach's point: Introduce this only after Man Coverage feels comfortable — it needs more field awareness.
